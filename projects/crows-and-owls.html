Crows and Owls

A monochrome browser-based narrative game about atmosphere, cumulative choice, and the weight of watching. Built from scratch with HTML5 Canvas and vanilla JavaScript. Part of the Monochrome Narratives series.

Overview

Crows and Owls is a short narrative game played entirely in the browser. It uses monochrome visuals, sparse audio, and minimal interaction to create a contemplative experience about observation, presence, and the passing of time.

There are no health bars, no score, no fail state. The player moves through a sequence of scenes, making small choices that accumulate quietly. The game remembers what you chose — not to punish or reward, but to shape the tone of what comes next.

Why I Made This

I wanted to explore what a game feels like when you strip away almost everything. No colour. No UI chrome. No obvious mechanics. Just a landscape, some text, and the feeling that your presence matters.

Most narrative games signal their choices loudly — dialogue trees, branching paths, consequence warnings. I was interested in the opposite: choices that feel small in the moment but leave a trace. The kind of decisions you only notice in retrospect.

The monochrome palette wasn't an aesthetic limitation — it was a design choice. Removing colour forces every other element to carry more weight: composition, contrast, timing, sound.

Player Experience

The game opens on a dark field. A figure — you — stands at the edge. Text appears slowly. You read it, and then you choose: move forward, or wait.

Each scene is a composition: a landscape rendered on canvas, ambient sound layered through Howler.js, and narrative text that responds to your accumulated choices. Some scenes are long. Some are a single line. The pacing is deliberate — silence is part of the design.

By the end, the world you've moved through reflects the pattern of your choices. Not dramatically — subtly. A different sky. A different sound. A line of text that could have been something else.

Key Features

  • Cumulative choice system — decisions don't branch the story; they shift its tone. The game tracks a quiet internal state that colours the narrative without exposing the mechanics.
  • Procedural monochrome landscapes — each scene is rendered on HTML5 Canvas with layered procedural elements: horizon lines, treelines, atmospheric gradients, particle systems for weather.
  • Spatial audio design — Howler.js manages layered ambient tracks that crossfade based on scene state. Wind, distant crows, silence — each is placed intentionally.
  • Typographic pacing — text appears character by character with variable timing. Pauses are written into the script, not just the rendering. Punctuation breathes differently.
  • Minimal interaction model — no inventory, no menus, no HUD. Interaction is reduced to movement and occasional binary choices presented as text.
  • Responsive canvas — the game scales to any viewport without breaking composition. Landscape ratios adapt, and text positioning is relative to the scene geometry.

Technical Architecture

The entire game runs in the browser with zero dependencies beyond Howler.js for audio. No build tools, no frameworks — just vanilla JavaScript and a canvas element.

Crows and Owls ├── engine/ │ ├── canvas.js — Rendering pipeline, scene composition, layering │ ├── scenes.js — Scene graph, transitions, state management │ ├── choices.js — Cumulative choice tracker, tone calculation │ └── typewriter.js — Character-by-character text renderer with pacing ├── audio/ │ ├── manager.js — Howler.js wrapper, crossfade logic, spatial mixing │ └── tracks/ — Ambient loops, one-shots, silence layers ├── data/ │ ├── script.json — Narrative content, choice definitions, pacing marks │ └── scenes.json — Scene definitions, visual parameters, audio mappings ├── render/ │ ├── landscape.js — Procedural horizon, treeline, gradient generation │ ├── weather.js — Particle systems: rain, fog, ash │ └── figures.js — Minimal character silhouettes └── index.html — Single entry point, fullscreen canvas

The rendering pipeline uses requestAnimationFrame with a scene-based compositor. Each scene defines its visual layers, and the engine renders them in order with blending. Scene transitions use opacity crossfades timed to the audio.

Reflections

Building Crows and Owls taught me that restraint is its own kind of complexity. Removing colour, UI elements, and explicit mechanics didn't simplify the project — it raised the bar for everything that remained. Every pixel of contrast, every millisecond of text timing, every decibel of ambient sound had to earn its place.

The cumulative choice system was the most interesting technical challenge. Traditional branching creates an exponential content problem. The tone-shifting approach let me create the feeling of meaningful choice without needing dozens of alternate paths — the same scene, described differently, feels like a different world.

This project also solidified my interest in the intersection of software engineering and experience design. The technical work — canvas rendering, audio management, state tracking — was in service of an emotional goal. That's the kind of engineering I find most rewarding.
