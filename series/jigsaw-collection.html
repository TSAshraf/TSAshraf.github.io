Pedagogical Software

Jigsaw Collection

An evolving project in software for learning through attention. What began as a dissertation jigsaw for museums has grown into a broader exploration of how software can encourage looking closely — across artefacts, artwork, and the spaces that hold them.

Presented as a series of case studies on the engineering and design decisions across four years of work — the alternatives considered, what didn't work, and what the project taught about building tools for learning.

Through-lines

What stays constant

Pedagogy

A puzzle that encourages looking closely rather than solving quickly. Context revealed through progress, not delivered upfront. Accessibility treated as a primary requirement — not a bolt-on. A deliberate refusal of leaderboards and other competitive incentives that pull attention away from the artefact. The goal is time spent with an image, not time saved.

Architecture

A content-agnostic puzzle engine — the same core mechanics generalise across artefacts, artwork, and architectural views. A local-first instinct, present in the dissertation and formalised in the classroom case study. Minimal UI that doesn't compete with the image. The platform changes — Java desktop, browser, native shell — but the underlying interaction model and design principles carry through.
