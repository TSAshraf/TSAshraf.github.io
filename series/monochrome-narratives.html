Monochrome Narratives

A series of atmospheric browser-based narrative games. Each is built from scratch with HTML5 Canvas and vanilla JavaScript — no frameworks, no engines. They share a monochrome palette, minimal interaction, and a focus on mood over mechanics.

The constraint is deliberate: removing colour forces every other element — composition, timing, sound, text — to carry more weight. These are short, self-contained works meant to be experienced rather than won.

4 games released · more in development

The Layer Below

A rescue excavation. You dig through layers of earth across a series of days, uncovering fragments and making decisions about what to preserve, what to document, and what to leave behind. The site tells its own story — if you're paying attention.

Terminus

A looping train game. You wake aboard with six passengers and no memory of boarding. Each loop resets the world but preserves what you've done. Throw someone from the carriage and they come back changed. Do nothing and the game notices. The only colour in the series appears when someone falls.

Crows and Owls

A narrative game about atmosphere, cumulative choice, and the weight of watching. You move through a sequence of monochrome landscapes, making small decisions that shape the tone of what comes next. There are no fail states — only traces of what you chose.

Technical Approach

How these are built

Shared principles

Every game in the series is built with vanilla JavaScript and HTML5 Canvas — no game engines, no frameworks. Each runs as a static site with zero build steps. Audio is managed through Howler.js or the Web Audio API. The constraint is intentional: keeping the stack minimal keeps the creative focus on the experience itself.

What varies

Each game explores a different interaction model. Crows and Owls uses cumulative tone-shifting through binary choices. The Layer Below uses spatial inspection across a day-based structure. Still Life uses Pretext — a DOM-free text layout engine — to render all narrative text directly on Canvas. Terminus adds persistent loop state, population decay, and tiered dialogue across six passengers — and is the only game in the series to break the monochrome constraint with colour. The shared palette and minimal UI create series cohesion, while the underlying systems are distinct.
